Hover the bar to sample the stress tensor and traction at the cursor.

This example illustrates the stress tensor and the Cauchy stress theorem t = P·n on a 2D iron bar under an axial load. The bar uses a simple uniaxial-stress model (linear elasticity with a Poisson-driven lateral contraction), so the only nonzero stress component is the axial one σ = F / A.

The first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor here is the diagonal matrix P = diag(σ, 0): only the axial component is nonzero, since the lateral surfaces are stress-free. The Poisson ratio affects only the deformation (the visible lateral contraction), not the stress. The bar is drawn with its reference outline (dashed) and deformed outline so the Poisson contraction is visible.

You configure a plane normal n by its angle. The dashed line is the plane itself (perpendicular to n); the region on the positive side of the plane is tinted. The traction t = P·n is the force per unit area that the material on the positive side exerts on the material on the negative side across that plane. When you hover, t (green) is drawn together with its decomposition into the normal component tn = (t·n) n (blue) and the shear component ts = t − tn (orange) relative to the plane. Rotate the normal and notice how a purely axial stress produces both a normal and a shear component on an inclined plane — this is exactly why stress is a tensor and not a scalar.

Enable Slow motion to watch the traction and its decomposition grow from zero to their full length over about a second each time you move the cursor, making the build-up of the normal and shear components easy to follow.

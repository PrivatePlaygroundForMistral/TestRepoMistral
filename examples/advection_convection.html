Advection

Advection is the transport of a quantity by a velocity field: the quantity is carried along with the flow, without any smoothing or spreading of its own. For a scalar T advected by velocity u:

∂T/∂t + u · ∇T = 0

In a uniform flow a blob of heat simply translates — its shape is (ideally) preserved. Try it here: set Buoyancy and Heater power to 0, give Wind a positive value, click to inject a blob, and watch it drift rigidly across the canvas.

Diffusion & convection

Real fluids also conduct heat. Adding the diffusive term (with thermal diffusivity κ) gives the advection–diffusion equation, which is what transport of heat in a moving fluid — convection — is built from:

∂T/∂t + u · ∇T = κ ∇²T

Raise Thermal diffusivity and the drifting blob now also blurs and spreads as it moves. Heat pipes, heat exchangers and weather are all governed by this combination: sharp edges are smeared by diffusion while the bulk is carried by the flow.

Natural (buoyancy-driven) convection

Fluids expand when warmed, so hot fluid is lighter and rises while cool fluid sinks — the flow is generated by the very quantity being transported. This feedback loop is natural convection. Here it is modeled with the Boussinesq approximation: density variations are ignored everywhere except in gravity, where each cell feels a force proportional to its temperature anomaly:

f = -β (T − T₀) g      // warm fluid is pushed up (canvas +y is down)

Restore Buoyancy and Heater power: the plume above the heater rises on its own, cools at the top surface, sinks at the sides and returns along the bottom — a convection cell (the mechanism behind Rayleigh–Bénard convection, sea breezes and boiling water). Contrast this with the wind case: there the velocity field was prescribed and independent of T; here T itself creates and drives u.

Numerics

The solver is a compact incompressible-fluid scheme per step:

  • Forces: buoyancy from T, then a small linear drag.
  • Diffusion: an explicit Laplacian for velocity (viscosity) and for T (conductivity), with the coefficient clamped so the explicit step stays stable at any time step.
  • Pressure projection: a Jacobi solve for pressure enforces ∇ · u = 0 (incompressibility), which is what closes the loop and turns local rising motion into large-scale circulating cells.
  • Semi-Lagrangian advection: for each grid cell, trace back along the velocity for one time step and copy (bilinearly interpolated) the value found there. Unconditionally stable, so no CFL blow-up at large time steps; the price is a little numerical diffusion, which you can counter by lowering the time step.

The ghost-cell boundary conditions are: solid free-slip walls for the velocity, insulating walls for heat at the sides and bottom, and a fixed cold temperature at the top.

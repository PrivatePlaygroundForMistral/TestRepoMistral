The left end is clamped. Gravity pulls the free right end down; it swings and bounces back, decaying through the beam's bending modes. Hover the bar to sample the stress tensor and traction t = σ·n.

A horizontal rod is clamped at the left end (fixed position and slope) and otherwise free. It is discretized into N elements along its length and integrated with a finite element force model plus semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler. The default material is a soft rubber (low Young's modulus) so the bend and swing are clearly visible; raise E toward ~100–200 GPa for a stiff metal rod (the motion then becomes small and fast).

v(t + dt) = v(t) + dt * a(t)        // update velocity with the current acceleration
x(t + dt) = x(t) + dt * v(t + dt)  // position uses the NEW velocity

Semi-implicit Euler uses the updated velocity for the position update, so it is symplectic and far better at conserving energy in oscillatory systems than plain explicit Euler — the swing and bounce of the bar stays bounded and only decays through the explicit damping term.

Each element resists axial stretch/compression through the Young's-modulus (membrane) energy ½ E A ε² and bending through a discrete Kirchhoff bending energy ½ E I κ² built from the second difference of the node positions, which penalizes curvature. Gravity is applied as a constant downward body force on every node. Because the left end is clamped, the torque from gravity bends the bar downward on the right; the elastic restoring forces swing it back, producing the damped oscillation you see.

The bar is colored by the local axial stress σ_xx (red for tension, blue for compression, white for zero). The 2D stress tensor at a sampled point combines the axial/bending stress on the xx component with the transverse shear σ_xy from the discrete bending force; hovering shows the traction t = σ·n (green) decomposed into its normal tn (blue) and shear ts (orange) components on the plane whose normal n you configure.

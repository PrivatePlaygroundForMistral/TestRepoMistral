Configure, then Start. The left end is clamped; gravity bends the free right end down and it swings/bounces back. Hover the bar to sample the stress tensor and traction t = σ·n. Mouse wheel rotates the plane normal.

A horizontal rod is clamped at the left end (fixed position and slope) and otherwise free. It is discretized into N finite elements along its length and integrated with semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler:

v(t + dt) = v(t) + dt * a(t)        // update velocity with the current acceleration
x(t + dt) = x(t) + dt * v(t + dt)  // position uses the NEW velocity

Using the updated velocity for the position update makes the integrator symplectic, so the oscillation stays bounded and decays only through the explicit damping term — far better for a swinging system than plain explicit Euler. Each step is sub-stepped to respect the CFL stability limit of the stiffest mode.

Internal forces come from a small finite element model: an axial (membrane) spring kA = E·A/dx and a discrete Kirchhoff bending stiffness kB = E·I/dx³ that penalizes curvature. Gravity is a constant downward body force on every node, so the free right end swings down and bounces back through the beam's bending modes. The default material is a soft rubber (low Young's modulus) so the bend and swing are clearly visible in real time; raise E toward ~100–200 GPa for a stiff metal rod (the motion then becomes small and very fast — enable Slow motion and lower the Time scale to see it).

Stability note. A stiff rod has a very high bending frequency, so a naive fixed-step integrator blows up (the bar “despawns” to NaN) the instant the per-step time exceeds the CFL limit. This example avoids that two ways: (1) the physical time advanced per rendered frame is dt · timeScale (slow-mo scales it down further), so the step stays well inside the CFL window; and (2) the per-frame sub-step count is capped, and any non-finite state is reset to rest, so the bar can never vanish. Configure everything, then press Start to launch the swing.

The bar is colored by the local axial/bending stress σ_xx (red for tension, blue for compression, white for zero; toggle Colors). The 2D stress tensor σ combines σ_xx with a transverse shear σ_xy. Hovering samples the tensor and shows the traction t = σ·n (green) decomposed into its normal tn (blue) and shear ts (orange) components on the plane whose normal n you configure (mouse wheel rotates it).
